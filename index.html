Why we’re running this

The series started as an idea from Aniket: invite people from different functions to share what they know in short sessions, so the team can learn beyond its immediate technical scope. Topics could include communication, leadership, HR functions and business processes.

The team was enthusiastic when the idea came up in a team meeting, so we ran a poll to find out what people most want to learn. Three areas drew the most interest.

Session 1 centres on two of them, with room for a short AI example. A deeper AI session could follow with another guest.

Who it’s forMost of the team is early in their careers: the majority at G6, a few at G8.

Agenda

A light first session: enough structure to leave room to read, think and talk, rather than covering too many things at once. This is the initial flow and it can be adjusted.

First 30 minutes

Read and reflect

  1. Introductions and context

    Why the series exists, who Hillary is, and what we expect from the session: everyone comes prepared and contributes.

  2. Reading and reflection

    Quiet, protected time with the same material for everyone, read right before the discussion so it is fresh. Write down questions, thoughts and situations you want to raise.

Why read first?Good intentions meet busy weeks. People skip preparation or have no questions ready, and the room goes quiet. A shared reading block gives everyone something to say.

Second 30 minutes

Discuss

  1. Core topic and experience sharing

    Hillary shares how she approaches the topic, drawing on real experience and on the questions people wrote down.

  2. Real-world scenario

    The group takes a first pass at an anonymised scenario. Hillary then walks through how she would approach it.

  3. Q&A and takeaways

    Open questions, with a goal for everyone to leave with at least one thing they can use in their own work.

How the session stays interactive

Questions are hard to produce on the spot, and a quiet room is easy to fall into. Each choice below gives people something to say and a reason to say it.

In the reading half

  • Same material for everyone

    Hillary prepares a custom playbook of topics, scenarios and examples. Everyone reads it right before the discussion, so it is fresh.

  • Protected time to think

    Read, reflect and write down questions and thoughts before anyone has to speak.

Question Thought Situation

In the discussion half

  • Real situations, details changed

    Bring something you are navigating now, with names and details altered, such as a team outside your own that is hard to work with. No venting about individuals.

  • First pass, then the walkthrough

    The group takes a scenario first. Where would you start? What does the data support? Whose perspective is missing? What can you move forward yourself, and what goes back to leadership with options and a recommendation? Then Hillary shows how she would approach it.

  • Ask the guest to go deeper

    Point at a part of the reading and ask Hillary to expand on it, so learning runs both ways.

Throughout

  • Prepared, so taking part is fair

    Everyone knows up front that they are expected to prepare and contribute. If someone is invited to speak, it is no surprise, and they have notes in front of them.

  • Light and focused

    A few things done well rather than many covered quickly.

  • Open to everyone

    Built for people who have never networked, approached a leader or asked a question in a session, so they get a first push to start, not only those who are already comfortable.

What the session could cover

Hillary offered several directions, most of them tied to the poll results. She is open to any or all of them, so the final mix is still to be agreed. Treat these as options, not a running order.

Leadership, influence and decision-making

Poll area
  • How senior leaders make decisions: how priorities are set, what leaders weigh, and how direction becomes what teams do.
  • How Hillary works with leadership and decides on the team’s behalf, bringing data, options and a recommendation.
  • How to decide and influence in your own role: where to start, what you can decide yourself, who to involve, and how to ask for help when direction is fuzzy.

Communication, presentation and collaboration

Poll area
  • How to prepare for a conversation with a manager, a senior leader, or board-level material. Most of the work happens before the meeting.
  • Who holds a piece of the story? Who needs to be aligned? What does this audience need to know, and what exactly are we asking them to decide?
  • Bringing it back to our work, such as asking a manager to pick between two priorities or another team to commit to a dependency.
  • One example told for different audiences, to show how the story and the ask change with who is in the room.

Unwritten rules of working life

  • Who owns this decision?
  • What belongs in a one-on-one, in scrum, or in a written update?
  • What to document, where it should live, and who needs to see it.
  • Protecting time to do the work, following up when something slips, and asking for help in a way that lets someone help you.
  • A gut check on meetings: ten people for an hour, with everyone’s time valued at $100 an hour, is a $1,000 meeting. What do we need to get out of it?

A look at the day to day

  • What it looks like to take the lead when an issue lands with partial information, several people each know a piece, and a decision is needed quickly.
  • Going from “I have no idea where to start” to “here’s what I know, here’s what I need to find out, and here’s what I’m doing first.”
  • Clear is kind: following up on agreed updates, raising what looks off while leaving room to be wrong, and ending with who owns what by when.

Scenarios and questions

Real situations the team could bring, and the questions people have said they want answered. Use them to shape the reading material and the worked example.

Situations to work through

  • Unclear ownership

    Handling a disagreement when it isn’t clear who owns what.

  • Conflicting priorities

    Managing the moment when different people expect different things from you.

  • Escalating

    Knowing when and how to escalate.

  • Visibility

    Making your contributions visible without constant self-promotion, giving others credit, and talking about your impact naturally.

  • Change

    Navigating changes in leadership, team structure, responsibilities or priorities.

  • Speaking up

    Raising a different view when everyone else seems aligned.

  • Alone or together

    Deciding when to act on your own and when to bring others in.

  • Asking for help

    What you have tried, when to dig in, and when to reach out.

  • Getting unstuck

    Getting started on an unfamiliar problem.

Questions the team wants answered

  • How do you handle competing priorities from multiple stakeholders?
  • How do you decide when ownership or direction isn’t clear?
  • How do you communicate an issue or recommendation so it gets the right attention?
  • How do you influence people and move work forward without the authority to decide?
  • How do you build visibility and become someone people trust?
  • How do you disagree respectfully and challenge an idea constructively?

Suggested worked example

Two people are asking me to do different things. How do I figure out which one takes priority?

Ground rules

The expectations to set when we introduce the series.

  • Come prepared

    Read the material and bring at least one question or thought.

  • Everyone contributes

    The session runs both ways, and its value depends on what each person brings.

  • Disagree well

    Different viewpoints and respectful challenge are welcome.

  • Leave with one thing

    Aim for at least one idea you can use in your own work.
